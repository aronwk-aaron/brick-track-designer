About

Brick Track Designer lays out LEGO-compatible train track (switches, crossings, crossovers, yards) and makes it into parts to 3D print: STL files cut to your print bed, joined like LEGO track. It runs in your browser: nothing you draw leaves your computer.

Free and open source (AGPL-3.0): source code and issues on GitHub. Made with three.js and the manifold geometry kernel.

Before printing a whole layout, print the test pieces (Templates › Test prints) and check the fits on your printer: what to print and what to look for.

Plan the rest of your layout with the Collaborative Brick Layout Designer: on the web at collab.aronwk.com (source), or the desktop app (source and downloads). ⬇ Layout parts gives it your printed track as parts.

Templates

  • Templates opens the template browser: a picture, a name and the size for each starting point, in sections. Type in the search box (every word must fit), or press a Kind, Gauge, Track or Radius button to narrow them; Clear undoes all of that.
  • A switch or crossing that comes in two hands is one card with Left and Right buttons. The star on a card keeps it in a Starred section at the top; the last eight you used are under Recent.
  • Press a card (or Enter on it; the arrow keys move between cards) to put it on the plan in place of the design. If the design has changes that aren't in a file, it asks first. Esc closes the browser.

Something looks wrong

  • Press Report a problem (in the 3D view's bar, or here: ). A zip downloads with the design, the settings, what the build said and the two views. Send it, or leave it in Downloads, so it can be looked at exactly as you saw it.
  • Open takes such a zip too and shows what was seen. Recent keeps the last 20 designs that built in this browser, so a problem seen an hour ago can still be reported.

A full circle

  • Full circle (under the routes) adds a closed ring of curve in equal pieces: pick its radius and how many pieces, and it says each piece's size and whether it fits the print bed. R40 and smaller default to 16 pieces, R56 to R120 to 32, bigger to 64. It's one route, and it builds as one part for each piece.
  • If a bigger piece needs it, Add raises Piece length (Settings › Print bed and cutting into parts) so the pieces stay whole. A piece that doesn't fit the bed is cut again: use more pieces.

Laying track

  • D or ✎ Draw, then click a grid point or a track's end to start; move to see the track; click to lay it and carry on.
  • Esc or a right-click stops drawing; Esc again goes back to Select.
  • Start on the middle of a track to branch off it: a switch.
  • With one track selected in Select, each curve has a round handle at its far end (drag it to change its angle, in 5.625° steps) and a diamond in its middle (drag it to change the radius, to the usual 24 + 8k), and each straight a square at its end (its length, in whole studs). Shift makes the steps finer; the value shows by the handle, with how many such curves make a circle; Esc puts it back. One Undo takes back the whole drag.
  • Drag a track's end to reshape it; drop it on another end to join them. Esc while dragging puts it back.

Routes by numbers

  • Each track is a route, with a card in the Routes panel (click its line to open it): its Shape (straights and curves, each to change, remove or add to), its Place (name, start, heading, height, and its own gauge where it isn't the design's), its Ends (an end connector or a buffer stop; a branch's lever side), and Guard rails and dual gauge. + Route adds one by numbers.
  • Mixed gauges: Settings › Track › Gauge is the design's gauge; a route's own Gauge (its card › Place; empty: the design's) makes that one track narrower or wider, a narrow-gauge line in a standard-gauge layout, say. Tracks of different gauges cross (each one's flangeways cut through the other's rails) and run side by side; points between them aren't built (the build says where). Only track at 4 studs is ever bought LEGO or BrickTracks track.
  • Each number's range is in its tip (hover it) and under the card's fields; a number outside it is held to it.

Switches

  • Points (yellow squares): click to take them out (both tracks' rails run through as plain track: a grey square), again to put them back; drag to move them along. A hollow yellow square is a track end a little off another track: click it to force points there (dashed: too far off; redraw it to start on the track).
  • Levers (orange): drag one across the track to swap its side, or further out (on tines, along too). Hover a lever to see what set its side, the branch it throws picked out in orange: a lever dragged across wins, then the branch route's own Lever side (on its card), then the Settings' Lever side.
  • The Trackwork panel has each one's numbers, and a three-way's branches one at a time; also the check rails (to lengthen, move in or take out) and the flangeway cuts (to lengthen). What you change by hand is marked Edited, and Reset puts it back.
  • Edits go with their track when it's moved, turned or mirrored. One whose trackwork is gone (its track deleted) is listed there as Left over, to remove.

Bought track: the parts palette

  • The Parts palette (under the routes) lays LEGO's and BrickTracks' track pieces one at a time: on the end of the selected route, or as a new route with nothing selected. ↶ and ↷ lay a curve to the left or the right. A switch's branch is a route of its own.
  • With a route selected, "Switch laid from its" picks which end of a switch goes on the route's end: the toe (the blades), the end of the straight or the end of the branch. The route then goes on through the switch to its toe, and the other way out of the toe is a new route. That is how the far switch of a passing loop is laid.
  • Track that's exactly one of those pieces is bought, not printed (green and purple in the 3D view), as Settings › Bought track says; on ballast, the ballast under it is printed.

Tables

  • The Tables panel sets out the tables or modules the layout stands on: + Rectangle, + Curved, + Any shape, or a ready-made one. Each has its numbers in the panel; drag one by its name on the plan (in Select).
  • The track is cut where one table meets the next (with Long track cut into: Track pieces, in Settings › Print bed and cutting into parts), and the parts list says what goes on each table.

Settings

  • Settings (, or the toolbar) has everything about how the pieces are made: track, ballast, underside, the switches' points and throws, the print bed and cutting, bought track, joints, labels, fit, monorail. Find a setting… searches them; each section folds.
  • Each setting shows its range and default. The round i by a setting shows pictures of its choices side by side. One that can't be changed now is greyed out and says why. Reset to defaults is at the bottom.

The 3D view, and building

  • The 3D view shows the parts as they'll print. With Auto on (in its bar) it builds after every change; with Auto off, when you press Build. Stop ends a long build; the view then shows the last design built, greyed, until Rebuild.
  • Drag turns it, the wheel zooms, a double-click (or Frame) fits it; Top and Underside look from above and below. Track, Bought, Points, Throws, Levers and Supports show or hide those parts; Explode pulls the parts apart. P hides the view (nothing is built while it's hidden).
  • The line under it says what was built (parts to print, bought pieces, size) and any warnings; a red ring on the plan and in the view marks where something's wrong.

Saving, and the downloads

  • The design is kept in this browser as you work. Save (Ctrl+S) downloads it as a file, asking its name the first time (Shift+click or Ctrl+Shift+S: under another name); Open (Ctrl+O) loads one. A template or a file taking the place of a design with changes not in a file asks first.
  • With the app open in two tabs or windows, the one you're not working in says the design was changed elsewhere, and asks which to keep.
  • STL downloads the parts to print (a zip when there are several, with a README.txt: what each file is, how to print it, how the points go together). Parts list shows what to buy and what to print (by table, if there are tables), and downloads the list with a BrickLink wanted list of the LEGO parts. The downloads are of the design as last built: they wait for a build to finish.

On a touch screen

  • One finger does what the mouse's left button does: tap a track to select it, drag it to move it, drag an end, a lever or points; in Draw, tap to lay track. In Select, a finger on empty ground moves the plan. Two fingers pinch to zoom and move it.
  • Hold a finger still on a track to add it to the selection, or take it out. The selection's bar has Copy, Paste, Mirror, Turn and Delete; the routes and settings panels slide over the plan from the toolbar's buttons.

Layout parts (for the layout designer)

  • ⬇ Layout parts downloads a zip with a parts library for the Collaborative Brick Layout Designer (and BlueBrick): one part for each printed track part, as cut for your print bed (the points, throws and levers drawn on the part they go in), with connection points where the track leaves it, and a set that puts them all down as designed. Bought LEGO track (straights, R40 curves, switches) is placed in the set as the library's own parts, not made again; a bought piece on printed ballast, and BrickTracks' pieces, are parts in the zip as the printed ones are.
  • Save the design under a name first: the folder, the parts and the set are named after it, so each design's parts are its own.
  • In the layout designer (desktop 1.4.0 or later): Tools > Parts > Add Parts from a Zip…, and choose the zip. The set is under the design's name in the Parts panel: place the whole set (its pieces are hidden in the library; Ungroup it to move them one at a time). After exporting again, add the new zip the same way. "Upload to server…" shares the parts and the set. (BlueBrick: copy the folder into its parts folder.) The README in the zip says more.

View and keys

  • Wheel zooms; right/middle-drag or Space+drag pans (while drawing too).
  • Select: click a track; Shift+click adds more; drag on empty ground for a box; Ctrl+A all; Esc none. Ctrl+C/X/V copy, cut, paste (at the mouse), Ctrl+D duplicate, M mirror, R / Shift+R turn 90°, Del delete.
  • D draw, S select, F fit, P 3D preview, , settings, ? this help, Ctrl+S save, Ctrl+O open, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redo.
