About

Brick Track Designer lays out LEGO-compatible train track (switches, crossings, crossovers, yards) and makes it into parts to 3D print: STL files cut to your print bed, joined like LEGO track. It runs in your browser: nothing you draw leaves your computer.

Free and open source (AGPL-3.0): source code and issues on GitHub. Made with three.js and the manifold geometry kernel.

Before printing a whole layout, print the test pieces (Templates › Test prints) and check the fits on your printer: what to print and what to look for.

How to print the parts (material, which way up, what breaks off) and how to put them together, a switch step by step: Printing and assembly.

Plan the rest of your layout with the Collaborative Brick Layout Designer: on the web at collab.aronwk.com (source), or the desktop app (source and downloads). ⬇ Layout parts gives it your printed track as parts.

Templates

  • Templates opens the template browser: a picture, a name and the size for each starting point, in sections. Type in the search box (every word must fit), or press a Kind, Gauge, Track or Radius button to narrow them; Clear undoes all of that.
  • A switch or crossing that comes in two hands is one card with Left and Right buttons. The star on a card keeps it in a Starred section at the top; the last eight you used are under Recent.
  • Press a card (or Enter on it; the arrow keys move between cards) to put it on the plan in place of the design. If the design has changes that aren't in a file, it asks first. Esc closes the browser.

Something looks wrong

  • Press Report a problem (in the 3D view's bar, or here: ). A zip downloads with the design, the settings, what the build said and the two views. Send it, or leave it in Downloads, so it can be looked at exactly as you saw it.
  • Open takes such a zip too and shows what was seen. Recent keeps the last 20 designs that built in this browser, so a problem seen an hour ago can still be reported.

A full circle

  • Full circle (under the routes) adds a closed ring of curve in equal pieces: pick its radius and how many pieces, and it says each piece's size and whether it fits the print bed. R40 and smaller default to 16 pieces, R56 to R120 to 32, bigger to 64. It's one route, and it builds as one part for each piece.
  • If a bigger piece needs it, Add raises Piece length (Settings › Print bed and cutting into parts) so the pieces stay whole. A piece that doesn't fit the bed is cut again: use more pieces.

Laying track

  • D or ✎ Draw, then click a grid point or a track's end to start; move to see the track; click to lay it and carry on.
  • Split and merge: with one track selected, S with the pointer on it (or the Split button, then a click on it) cuts it in two at the nearest whole stud, at least a stud in from either end; the two halves keep everything that was on that part (guard rails, third rail, ends, hand edits, heights). Select two tracks that meet end to end and press Merge to make them one.
  • Cuts into parts (expert): the app cuts long track into parts itself (Settings › Cutting). To choose where, open a track's card, Cuts into parts, press Add a cut…, then click on the track: it is cut there, a whole stud along, with an ordinary track end each side, and stays one route. Each cut is a number in the card (studs from the track's start, at least 2 in from its ends and 2 apart); ✕ takes it out. Only these cuts: the app adds none of its own on that track. Keep the cuts it has now: makes the app's cuts the track's own, so later changes don't move them. Automatic cuts: back to the app's. Cuts are shown on the plan as orange lines across the track (dashed while the app may add its own). A cut is not made in points or a crossing, nor where the track is one part with a track beside it: the warnings say which. Hand cuts need Long track cut into: Track pieces. They go with the track when it is moved, turned, split, joined or reshaped; one left past the end of a shortened track is kept in the card, marked, and not made.
  • Cut paths (expert): where tracks are one part (points, a crossover, a yard's throat) no ordinary track end can be made, so the cut is drawn. Open Cut paths (left panel), press Add a cut path…, then click its points on the plan: they snap to the corners between studs, so the cut runs between the studs and never through one; a slant is laid as steps. Start outside the part or on another cut, end outside it or on another cut, and press Enter (Backspace takes the last point back, Esc cancels). A path may run along a track's centre line, between two tracks, or across them, and round a frog. It cuts every part it crosses from edge to edge; the two sides join as Settings › Parts join with says, or as the path's own Joint in the panel. Click a path to select it: drag a corner or the whole path, Delete removes it. Not made, and drawn red with the reason: through a blade's moving length, within 2 studs of a frog, down a rail, less than 2 studs beside another cut, ending inside a part, leaving a sliver or a part in two. A path goes with its tracks when they are all moved, turned or mirrored together; if only some move, it stays. Cuts across one track (its card's Cuts into parts) can be clicked and dragged along the track on the plan too. Cut paths need Long track cut into: Track pieces.
  • Settings of its own (expert): a track can have its own ballast or sleepers (and their width and gap), studs, underside and grip, and be bought or printed, over the design's settings. Open its card: Bed and underside, and Bought track under Cuts into parts; a track with a banked curve has its ramps' settings under Shape. Each says the design's value (Design default: …) until you choose one; then the design's is said under it, with Use the design default. Choosing the design's own value is the same as not choosing. Tracks that are one part (they meet in points or a crossing, their beds are within 2 studs, or one passes within a lever's reach of another's points) share these: the first of them in the list with its own wins, and a warning names both. Tracks joined end to end are two parts, so one can be on a solid bed and the next on sleepers. In Settings, a setting some tracks have their own of says which. (Tracks built apart aren't checked against each other for running over each other: tracks that close are one part anyway.) Ballast lifts the track a plate (3.2 mm), with its buddy layer two: a track without it beside one with it wants plates under it.
  • Stretches (expert): part of a track can have its own of the same settings. In the track's card, Bed and underside, press Add a stretch…, then press on the track on the plan where the stretch starts and drag along it to where it ends (whole studs, at least 2 long; Esc cancels); then set what it has in the card, where its from and to can be typed too (whole studs from the track's start); each setting says the track's (As the track: …) until you choose one. The track is cut at a stretch's ends, with an ordinary track end each side, and stays one route; no piece under 2 studs is left beside a stretch (it is built to the track's end or the stretch before instead). Stretches go with the track when it is turned, split, merged or reshaped; one left past the end of a shortened track is kept, said so, and not built. A selected track's stretches are drawn with an orange line. On the plan, click a stretch of the selected track to select it, then drag it, or one of its ends, along the track: it moves on whole studs and stops at the track's ends and at the stretch beside it, and says why it stopped. The numbers in the card follow. Delete takes the selected stretch out. Each is one undo step.
  • Expert tools (the button at the top of the routes panel; off to start with, remembered in this browser, not kept in the design): shows what an expert edits one by one, on the plan and in the cards: cuts across a track, cut paths, stretches, and a track's own settings (the cards' Bed and underside and Cuts into parts, the Cut paths panel, the Pick list in the legend). Hidden, nothing in the design changes and it is built the same: cuts and stretches stay drawn on the plan, a click picks the track under them, and a track that has any says so on its card ("Has 2 cuts by hand, 1 stretch") with Show, which turns the tools on. The things marked (expert) below need them shown.
  • Selecting a cut, a cut path, a stretch or a joint (expert): click it on the plan in Select. The bar over the plan says what it is ("Cut across T1, 12 studs from its start") and has only what can be done with it, and its card comes over the routes' cards with its numbers to type: a cut's studs from the track's start, a stretch's from and to, a cut path's corners (x, y in studs). Delete (or Delete on the bar or the card) takes it out, the arrow keys move it a stud (Shift: eight; a cut or a stretch along its track, the way the arrow points), Esc lets go of it. Each is one undo step. A click on a track or on empty ground lets go of it; tracks and tables are selected as ever.
  • Clicks pick (expert; in the legend): what a click on the plan takes in Select. Everything, to start with: whatever is under the pointer. Tracks and tables; Cuts and cut paths; Stretches (every track's are drawn and can be picked, not only the selected track's); Joints (a click on a joint selects it, also a branch's under its points); Points, levers, check rails. With one kind picked a click takes only that and leaves the rest alone, and the hint line says which. Remembered in this browser.
  • Joined tracks: two tracks are joined where an end of one lies on the other's end, or on its side, in line with it (a dot on the plan; an end joined to nothing has a small ring). Nothing is set: tracks that meet are joined. Drag a track, nudge it with the arrow keys, mirror or turn it, and every track joined to it goes along. Hold Alt while dragging to move it alone: the joints it leaves are ringed in red and the hint line says "Took T3 apart from T2". Reshape a track (an end, a handle, a number in its card) and what is joined to it past the change goes with it; where that can't be (a loop), the track still takes its new shape, the joint at its changed side opens, and the hint line says which. A branch stays on the track it leaves. Double-click a track to select it and all that is joined to it. Click a joined end to select the joint, or select just the two tracks (a branch's joint is under its points, which take the click): Take apart (selection bar) leaves the two touching but each on its own (an orange ring); select it again and Join puts them back. Tracks taken apart and then moved off are simply two tracks: put back together they are joined again. A locked track holds what is joined to it. Tracks that only cross are not joined. Every joint is also a row in the Trackwork panel, under Joints between tracks: the two tracks, where it is, and whether it is joined or taken apart. Hover the row to see it ringed on the plan, click it to select the joint there, and Take apart or Join in the row does the same as on the bar (a branch's joint is easiest reached this way).
  • Parallel…: with one track selected, a new track beside it, 6 to 200 studs over (default 16; 8 and 16 are the usual), to its left or right: straights as long, curves with the radius concentric with the original. It says why not where a curve would go under R24.
  • Lock: tick Locked on a route's card (or a table's) and it can still be selected and copied, but not dragged, nudged, reshaped by its handles or ends, mirrored, turned, split, merged or deleted; the plan shows a small padlock on it, and the hint line says "R3 is locked" when you try. A copy isn't locked.
  • Templates: click a card to add it to your design (or, with an empty design, to open it); Shift+click does the other; the small Add and Open under a card say the same. A card with a left and a right hand has a Left | Right switch. Adding: the template follows the pointer, its open ends snap onto the design's open ends (hold Alt for no snapping), R / Shift+R turn it by 22.5°, M mirrors it, a click puts it down, Esc cancels. With one route selected it starts on that route's open end. Enter on a card does its click, Shift+Enter the other. It's added with this design's settings; a template whose settings differ says so.
  • My templates: Save as template… (in the template browser, or on the selection bar to keep just the selected tracks and tables) keeps a design in this browser under a name; it's listed first in Templates, with Open, Add to design, Rename, Export (a .btd.json file), Delete and Import… (reads .btd.json files). The saved settings come with it. If the browser won't keep anything the group says so; there's room for 60 templates or about 1 MB.
  • Measure: T or the Measure button, then click two points (they snap to track ends and centre lines): the distance in studs and mm, and the angle between the tracks there. Esc clears. Not saved. (M is Mirror.)
  • The plan by keyboard: Tab to the plan (it shows a focus ring). [ and ] pick the previous or next route. With a route selected, Tab moves between its handles and the arrow keys change the one you're on (↑/→ more, ↓/← less: a stud, or a degree for an angle; Shift: 8 studs, or 11.25°). Enter lays track on from its end (Shift+Enter: from its start): the arrow keys move a crosshair a stud (Shift: eight), Enter lays track there, Esc stops.
  • Guides: drawing, or dragging a track's end, the point snaps to the line straight on from another track's end, to 8 and 16 studs beside a straight, to a whole number of studs straight on, and (Curves: free) to the usual radii. A dashed line and a small label give the value (studs, degrees, radius). Hold Alt for no snapping (the stud grid stays); it also stops the snap onto other tracks' ends.
  • Esc or a right-click stops drawing; Esc again goes back to Select.
  • Start on the middle of a track to branch off it: a switch.
  • With one track selected in Select, each curve has a round handle at its far end (drag it to change its angle, in 5.625° steps) and a diamond in its middle (drag it to change the radius, to the usual 24 + 8k), and each straight a square at its end (its length, in whole studs). Shift makes the steps finer; the value shows by the handle, with how many such curves make a circle; Esc puts it back. One Undo takes back the whole drag.
  • Drag a track's end to reshape it; drop it on another end to join them (only tracks with the same gauge, guard rails and third rail join). Esc while dragging puts it back: a track end, a handle, a whole track or a table.
  • The arrow keys move the selected tracks a stud (Shift: eight). F fits the selected tracks in view, or all of them with none selected.

Routes by numbers

  • Each track is a route, with a card in the Routes panel (click its line to open it): its Shape (straights and curves, each to change, remove or add to), its Place (name, start, heading, height, and its own gauge where it isn't the design's), its Ends (an end connector or a buffer stop; a branch's lever side), and Guard rails and dual gauge. + Route adds one by numbers.
  • Mixed gauges: Settings › Track › Gauge is the design's gauge; a route's own Gauge (its card › Place; empty: the design's) makes that one track narrower or wider, a narrow-gauge line in a standard-gauge layout, say. Tracks of different gauges cross (each one's flangeways cut through the other's rails) and run side by side; points between them aren't built (the build says where). Only track at 4 studs is ever bought LEGO or BrickTracks track.
  • Each number's range is in its tip (hover it) and under the card's fields; a number outside it is held to it.

Switches

  • Points (yellow squares): click to take them out (both tracks' rails run through as plain track: a grey square), again to put them back; drag to move them along. A hollow yellow square is a track end a little off another track: click it to force points there (dashed: too far off; redraw it to start on the track).
  • Levers (orange): drag one across the track to swap its side, or further out (on tines, along too). Hover a lever to see what set its side, the branch it throws picked out in orange: a lever dragged across wins, then the branch route's own Lever side (on its card), then the Settings' Lever side.
  • The Trackwork panel has each one's numbers, and a three-way's branches one at a time; also the check rails (to lengthen, move in or take out) and the flangeway cuts (to lengthen). What you change by hand is marked Edited, and Reset puts it back.
  • Edits go with their track when it's moved, turned or mirrored. One whose trackwork is gone (its track deleted) is listed there as Left over, to remove.

Bought track: the parts palette

  • The Parts palette (under the routes) lays LEGO's and BrickTracks' track pieces one at a time: on the end of the selected route, or as a new route with nothing selected. ↶ and ↷ lay a curve to the left or the right. A switch's branch is a route of its own.
  • With a route selected, "Switch laid from its" picks which end of a switch goes on the route's end: the toe (the blades), the end of the straight or the end of the branch. The route then goes on through the switch to its toe, and the other way out of the toe is a new route. That is how the far switch of a passing loop is laid.
  • Track that's exactly one of those pieces is bought, not printed (green and purple in the 3D view), as Settings › Bought track says; on ballast, the ballast under it is printed.

Tables

  • The Tables panel sets out the tables or modules the layout stands on: + Rectangle, + Curved, + Any shape, or a ready-made one. Each has its numbers in the panel; drag one by its name on the plan (in Select).
  • The track is cut where one table meets the next (with Long track cut into: Track pieces, in Settings › Print bed and cutting into parts), and the parts list says what goes on each table.

Settings

  • Settings (, or the toolbar) has everything about how the pieces are made: track, ballast, underside, the switches' points and throws, the print bed and cutting, bought track, joints, labels, fit, monorail. Find a setting… searches them; each section folds.
  • Each setting shows its range and default. The round i by a setting shows pictures of its choices side by side. One that can't be changed now is greyed out and says why. Reset to defaults is at the bottom.

The 3D view, and building

  • The 3D view shows the parts as they'll print. With Auto on (in its bar) it builds after every change; with Auto off, when you press Build. Stop ends a long build; the view then shows the last design built, greyed, until Rebuild.
  • Drag turns it, the wheel zooms, a double-click (or Frame) fits it; Top and Underside look from above and below. Track, Bought, Points, Throws, Levers and Supports show or hide those parts; Explode pulls the parts apart. P hides the view (nothing is built while it's hidden).
  • The line under it says what was built (parts to print, bought pieces, size) and any warnings; a red ring on the plan and in the view marks where something's wrong.

Saving, and the downloads

  • The design is kept in this browser as you work. Save (Ctrl+S) downloads it as a file, asking its name the first time (Shift+click or Ctrl+Shift+S: under another name); Open (Ctrl+O) loads one. A template or a file taking the place of a design with changes not in a file asks first.
  • With the app open in two tabs or windows, the one you're not working in says the design was changed elsewhere, and asks which to keep.
  • STL downloads the parts to print (a zip when there are several, with a README.txt: what each file is, how to print it, how the points go together). Parts list shows what to buy and what to print (by table, if there are tables), and downloads the list with a BrickLink wanted list of the LEGO parts. The downloads are of the design as last built: they wait for a build to finish.

On a touch screen

  • One finger does what the mouse's left button does: tap a track to select it, drag it to move it, drag an end, a lever or points; in Draw, tap to lay track. In Select, a finger on empty ground moves the plan. Two fingers pinch to zoom and move it.
  • Hold a finger still on a track to add it to the selection, or take it out. The selection's bar has Copy, Paste, Mirror, Turn and Delete; the routes and settings panels slide over the plan from the toolbar's buttons.

Layout parts (for the layout designer)

  • ⬇ Layout parts downloads a zip with a parts library for the Collaborative Brick Layout Designer (and BlueBrick): one part for each printed track part, as cut for your print bed (the points, throws and levers drawn on the part they go in), with connection points where the track leaves it, and a set that puts them all down as designed. Bought LEGO track (straights, R40 curves, switches) is placed in the set as the library's own parts, not made again; a bought piece on printed ballast, and BrickTracks' pieces, are parts in the zip as the printed ones are.
  • Save the design under a name first: the folder, the parts and the set are named after it, so each design's parts are its own.
  • In the layout designer (desktop 1.4.0 or later): Tools > Parts > Add Parts from a Zip…, and choose the zip. The set is under the design's name in the Parts panel: place the whole set (its pieces are hidden in the library; Ungroup it to move them one at a time). After exporting again, add the new zip the same way. "Upload to server…" shares the parts and the set. (BlueBrick: copy the folder into its parts folder.) The README in the zip says more.

View and keys

  • Wheel zooms; right/middle-drag or Space+drag pans (while drawing too).
  • Select: click a track; Shift+click adds more; drag on empty ground for a box; Ctrl+A all; Esc none. Ctrl+C/X/V copy, cut, paste (at the mouse), Ctrl+D duplicate, M mirror, R / Shift+R turn 90°, Del delete.
  • D draw, S select, F fit, P 3D preview, , settings, ? this help, Ctrl+S save, Ctrl+O open, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redo.
